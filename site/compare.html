Binder compared

Other ways to start your PC games, side by side, so you can pick the one that fits. Binder is new, small and does less than most of them; this page tries to say so plainly.

Checked in November 2026 against each maker's own pages, which change. Not checked means it couldn't be confirmed there, not that the feature is missing; No statement found means the maker hasn't said, not that AI isn't used. If something here is wrong or out of date, open an issue on GitHub.

In short

Playnite
If you want one library for many stores, emulators, themes, extensions and a full-screen controller mode, for free and open source. It has been around for years, with many community themes and add-ons.
GOG GALAXY 2.0
If you want games and friends from several platforms in one place, including games you own but haven't installed, tied to a GOG account.
LaunchBox
If emulation and retro collections matter most, or you're building an arcade or TV setup with Big Box.
Xbox app
If you already use it: on current Windows it lists installed games from other stores too, with nothing else to install.
Steam
If nearly all your games are on Steam: the rest can be added by hand, and Big Picture works with a controller.
Windows alone
If Start search and a few desktop shortcuts are enough. Nothing to install, nothing running.
Binder
If you want a small panel of your installed games that closes while you play, tidies away desktop shortcuts and can optionally tune Windows for the game. Expect fewer features and a young project.

Is Binder right for you?

It may suit you if

  • your games are installed from Steam, Epic, GOG, the EA app, Ubisoft Connect, the Xbox app or Rockstar, on one Windows PC;
  • you want game shortcuts off your desktop, with a way to put them back;
  • you want the launcher itself gone while you play, with only a helper of about 3 MB waiting;
  • you'd like Windows tuned while a game runs and put back afterwards, with every change documented;
  • you want open source software that needs no account and sends nothing about you;
  • you're fine with a new project, made by one person, with code written by an AI.

Something else may suit you better if

  • you want games you own but haven't installed in the list: GOG GALAXY or Playnite;
  • you play emulated or retro games: LaunchBox or Playnite;
  • you want themes, extensions or a full-screen mode for the TV: Playnite, LaunchBox's Big Box or Steam's Big Picture;
  • many of your games are on Battle.net, Riot, Amazon Games, itch.io, Humble or Meta: Playnite, or the Xbox app for Battle.net;
  • you want friends and achievements from several platforms in one place: GOG GALAXY;
  • you use a PlayStation or Switch controller: Steam's Big Picture or Playnite's Fullscreen mode, until Binder supports them;
  • you'd rather use software that has been around for years, or code reviewed by people: any of the others.

What Binder doesn't do

  • It's young. The first version came out in November 2026, and it's made by one person. Expect bugs and missing pieces.
  • Its code is written by an AI (Claude, by Anthropic) and isn't reviewed line by line by a human developer. Its author designs it and tests each version on real PCs.
  • The installer isn't code-signed, so Windows SmartScreen may warn before it runs.
  • Installed games only. Games you own but haven't installed don't appear.
  • Some stores aren't supported yet: Battle.net, Riot, Amazon Games, itch.io, Humble and Meta. Their games can be added by hand or through their desktop shortcuts.
  • No emulators, no themes, no add-ons, no full-screen mode, no friends or achievements.
  • Covers come only from Steam; other games get a plain cover made from their icon.
  • Xbox controllers only for now; PlayStation and Switch controllers are planned.
  • One PC. Your list and playtime stay on the PC; nothing syncs to other PCs.
  • Windows 10 and 11 (64-bit) only.

The same for all of them

Every option here, Binder included, starts a game through the store it belongs to. Steam, Epic and the others still have to be installed, and they still run while you play their games: that's where saves, updates and friends lists come from.
