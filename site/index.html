Your desktop, without the game shortcuts

Binder keeps the games from Steam, Epic, GOG, the other launchers and your desktop shortcuts in one panel by the clock. Click a cover to play. While you play, Binder closes.

Scroll to tidy this desktop

Your game shortcuts move into Binder

You tick which shortcuts are games. Binder moves them into its own folder and can put them back on the desktop at any time. Your other icons stay where they are.

Every launcher's games, by the clock

Binder finds what you've installed and keeps the list up to date. Open it with the icon next to the clock or Win + Shift + G, and click a cover to play.

Steam · Epic Games · GOG · EA app · Ubisoft Connect · Xbox app · Rockstar Games Launcher · your desktop shortcuts · any game you add

While you play, Binder is closed

Ten seconds after the game starts, Binder quits. Only a small helper stays to notice when the game ends. Once it has found the game, it just waits and uses no processor time.

When the game closes, Binder comes back

It adds the session to the game's playtime and shows it at the top. If you'd rather it stayed closed, turn this off in Settings.

  • A tidy desktop: Binder keeps the shortcuts you moved in its own folder and can put them back anytime.

Your data stays on your PC

Binder only connects to the internet to download cover art for Steam games, from Steam's servers, and once a day to look for a new version on GitHub (you can turn that off). It sends nothing about you or your PC, and installs only updates signed by its maker.

  • No account, no telemetry, no analytics
  • Installs for your account only, without admin rights1
  • Your library, playtime and settings are saved in your user folder (AppData)
  • Uninstall from Settings › Apps. Your shortcuts go back on the desktop, and you choose whether to keep your data

1 Binder and its installer run with your account's normal rights. Only Game mode's optional admin helper asks for administrator rights: once when you choose Allow… to let Game mode pause background services and set network priority, again if a Binder update changes the helper, and when you remove it or uninstall Binder. Frequently asked questions

What Binder changes on your PC, item by item, is in the documentation.

Will it slow down my games?

It shouldn't. Binder closes 10 seconds after the game starts. Only the helper keeps running, and it uses no processor time once it has found the game. Its memory is under How much it uses.

Does it delete my desktop shortcuts?

No. The shortcuts you choose are moved into Binder's own folder, and you can put them back anytime. Uninstalling Binder puts them back too.

Do I still need Steam, Epic and the others?

Yes. Binder starts each game through the launcher it belongs to, so saves, updates and friends lists work as before.

Why does Windows warn me when I run the installer?

The installer isn't code-signed yet, so SmartScreen may show "Windows protected your PC". If you downloaded it from this page, choose More info, then Run anyway.

What if Binder doesn't notice the game?

If the helper can't find the game within about 3 minutes, Binder opens again and tells you. You can then pick the game's process in its settings so it's found next time.

Which launchers aren't supported yet?

Battle.net, Riot, Amazon Games, itch.io, Humble and Meta are planned but not there yet. Until then, add those games yourself or import their desktop shortcuts. Controller support is also planned.

Where does Binder keep my data?

On your PC, in your user folder: your library, playtime, settings and imported shortcuts in %APPDATA%\Binder, and the cover pictures in %LOCALAPPDATA%\Binder. When you uninstall Binder, it asks whether to delete them.

What does it cost?

Nothing, it's free. Binder is open source under the GNU GPL, version 3: anyone can read, change and share the code on GitHub, and changed versions stay open too.
